Method

The cell is a four-compartment axisymmetric finite-strain model (Skalak cortex, near-incompressible neo-Hookean cytoplasm and nucleoplasm, nuclear envelope). For each device the aperture was closed quasi-statically around reference cells from first contact until the continuation stopped converging (at most a/R = 2.4), storing the deformed mesh and the confinement energy. Shapes depend on confinement and on the dimensionless groups, not on stiffness, so they are rescaled to the selected diameter; the reference with the closer nucleus-to-cell ratio is used (0.50 or 0.625). The energy collapses as W = Gsa²g(ρ/a), with Gs = Eappa/2.608. Channels use the axial reduction indexed by the neck radius; membranes use the work on the volume entering the pore. The liquid-drop bound, 2γ(1/R − 1/a), is independent of the finite-element model.

Limits

Quasi-static, axisymmetric and without resolved fluid, adhesion or friction. The passage animation uses the same approximation as the critical pressure: at each position the cell takes the computed state for the local wall radius, symmetric about its own centre, so near the neck its front can overlap the wall; an axial-advance finite-element model would remove this. Results above a/R = 1.636 are extrapolation; above the last converged state nothing is computed. With the soft cytoplasm of the model (Π = 0.1), strain concentrates in the layer between cortex and nucleus. Mechanical parameters of patient-derived CTCs have not been measured; their preset uses transferred values.
